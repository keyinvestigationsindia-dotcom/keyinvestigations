Evidence Handling Policy

Last updated: 18 May 2026

1. Purpose and Scope

This Evidence Handling Policy establishes the standards and procedures governing the collection, preservation, documentation, storage, and presentation of evidence by KEY Investigations. The policy is designed to ensure that all evidence gathered during insurance investigations maintains its integrity, authenticity, and admissibility in legal and quasi-legal proceedings.

This policy applies to all personnel involved in evidence collection and handling, including field investigators, surveillance operatives, QC reviewers, case managers, and administrative staff. Adherence to this policy is mandatory and non-compliance may result in disciplinary action.

2. Evidence Collection Standards

2.1 General Principles

  • All evidence must be collected using lawful methods in compliance with applicable Indian laws, including the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, the Information Technology Act, 2000, and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023
  • Investigators must obtain proper authorisation before commencing evidence collection, including client referral instructions and any necessary consent or legal permissions
  • The privacy and dignity of all individuals must be respected during evidence collection. Surveillance and documentation must comply with legal boundaries
  • Evidence must be collected objectively and without bias. Investigators must document both inculpatory and exculpatory findings
  • The investigator must note the date, time, location, weather conditions, and persons present at the time of evidence collection

2.2 Types of Evidence

Documentary Evidence

FIRs, medical records, repair invoices, policy documents, correspondence, government records, and any written or printed material relevant to the investigation. Original documents must be preserved; certified copies obtained where originals are unavailable.

Photographic and Video Evidence

Site photographs, vehicle damage documentation, surveillance recordings, hospital and premises photographs. Must include metadata (timestamp, GPS coordinates) and be captured according to the documentation standards in Section 5.

Testimonial Evidence

Statements from claimants, witnesses, neighbours, medical professionals, repair shop personnel, and other relevant parties. Must be recorded accurately and signed where possible.

Physical Evidence

Vehicle parts, damaged property samples, mechanical components, and other tangible items. Must be handled with care to prevent contamination or alteration.

Digital Evidence

Electronic records, social media content, GPS data, CCTV footage, call records, and digital communications. Subject to the digital integrity requirements in Section 4.

2.3 Field Collection Protocols

  • Investigators must carry valid identification and authorisation letters when conducting field visits
  • All interactions with claimants and witnesses must be professional, courteous, and non-coercive
  • Statements must be recorded in the language of the deponent and translated where necessary, with both versions preserved
  • Investigators must not alter, move, or tamper with evidence at scene locations; document the scene as found
  • If evidence is at risk of destruction or loss, the investigator must take immediate preservation steps and document the justification

3. Chain of Custody

Maintaining an unbroken chain of custody is essential for the evidentiary value of all materials collected. The chain of custody record must document every transfer, access, and handling of evidence from the moment of collection to its final disposition.

3.1 Required Documentation

For each item of evidence, the chain of custody record must include:

  • Unique evidence identifier (case number + sequential item number)
  • Description of the item, including distinguishing characteristics
  • Name and designation of the person who collected or received the item
  • Date, time, and location of collection or transfer
  • Purpose of each transfer (e.g., QC review, client submission, court production)
  • Method and condition of storage at each stage
  • Signature of the person releasing and the person receiving at each transfer point

3.2 Digital Chain of Custody

For digital evidence, the chain of custody is maintained through:

  • Automated logging in the client portal system, recording every access, download, and modification with user identity and timestamp
  • Hash verification at each transfer point to confirm file integrity
  • Immutable audit trails that cannot be altered or deleted by any user, including administrators
  • Version control for any evidence that undergoes authorised modification (e.g., redaction of unrelated personal data)

4. Digital Evidence Integrity

4.1 Hash Verification

All digital evidence files are assigned a SHA-256 cryptographic hash at the moment of capture or receipt. This hash serves as a digital fingerprint that can verify the file has not been altered since collection. Hash values are stored separately from the evidence files and are included in all evidence submission documentation.

4.2 Metadata Preservation

  • EXIF data on photographs (timestamp, GPS coordinates, device information) must be preserved and not stripped or modified
  • Video files must retain original encoding, timestamps, and metadata
  • Document metadata (creation date, author, modification history) must be preserved for electronic documents
  • Where metadata is absent or unreliable, the investigator must note this in the evidence record

4.3 Working Copies

Original digital evidence files are stored in read-only format and are never modified. All analysis, annotation, and report preparation is performed on verified working copies. The relationship between original files and working copies is documented, and working copies carry their own hash values.

4.4 Anti-Tampering Controls

  • Investigation devices (cameras, phones) used for evidence capture are configured to embed timestamps and location data automatically
  • Investigators are prohibited from using photo editing software, filters, or post-processing on evidence photographs
  • Screenshots of digital content (social media, websites) must include the URL bar, date/time, and be captured using company-approved screen recording tools
  • Any discrepancy between hash values at different points in the custody chain triggers an immediate integrity review

5. Photographic and Video Documentation Standards

5.1 Photographic Standards

  • All photographs must be captured in the highest resolution available on the device
  • GPS/location tagging must be enabled on the capture device at all times during evidence photography
  • A systematic approach must be followed: start with wide-angle establishing shots showing the overall scene, then proceed to medium shots for context, and close-up shots for detail
  • For vehicle investigations: capture all four sides, the roof, undercarriage (where accessible), dashboard/odometer, VIN plate, engine compartment, and all damage areas with scale reference
  • For property investigations: capture the exterior from all accessible angles, the interior of affected areas, close-ups of damage, and surrounding context
  • Include a reference object (scale ruler, coin, pen) in close-up photographs to establish size
  • Photographs must not be cropped, filtered, colour-adjusted, or otherwise modified after capture
  • Each photograph must be logged in the evidence register with a brief description of what it depicts

5.2 Video Documentation Standards

  • Video recordings must be captured in a minimum resolution of 1080p with audio enabled where relevant
  • The investigator should narrate the video where appropriate, stating the date, time, location, case reference, and describing what is being documented
  • Video should begin with a wide shot establishing the location and then move systematically through the subject matter
  • Surveillance video must be continuous and unedited; any interruptions must be documented with explanations
  • Video files must be transferred to secure storage within 24 hours of capture and not retained solely on the capture device

6. Storage and Retention

6.1 Digital Storage

  • All digital evidence is stored on encrypted servers (AES-256) with automated redundant backups
  • Access to stored evidence is controlled through role-based permissions with comprehensive audit logging
  • Storage systems are monitored for integrity using periodic hash verification checks
  • Backup copies are stored in geographically separate locations to protect against data loss

6.2 Physical Storage

  • Physical evidence is stored in a dedicated, locked, climate-controlled evidence room with restricted access
  • Entry to the evidence room is logged electronically, and the room is under continuous CCTV surveillance
  • Evidence items are individually packaged, labelled, and catalogued in the evidence management system
  • Perishable or environmentally sensitive evidence is stored under appropriate conditions with regular condition checks

6.3 Retention Periods

  • Active cases: All evidence retained for the duration of the investigation and any subsequent litigation
  • Closed cases (no litigation): Evidence retained for a minimum of 8 years from case closure
  • Cases involved in litigation: Evidence retained until final disposition of all legal proceedings, appeals, and enforcement actions, plus 3 additional years
  • Fraud cases: Evidence retained for a minimum of 10 years or as required by the relevant regulatory authority
  • Upon expiry of retention periods, evidence is destroyed securely: digital files are permanently deleted with certified wiping tools; physical evidence is destroyed under documented supervision

7. Court-Admissible Evidence Preparation

When investigation evidence may be presented in legal proceedings, the following additional standards apply to ensure admissibility under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023:

7.1 Documentation Requirements

  • Complete chain of custody documentation, signed and dated at every transfer point
  • Certificate under Section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act (or equivalent provision under BSA 2023) for all electronic evidence, issued by the person responsible for the computer or device
  • Affidavit from the investigating officer attesting to the authenticity and integrity of the evidence
  • Hash verification certificates confirming that digital evidence has not been altered since collection
  • Evidence index listing all items with descriptions, collection dates, and current storage location

7.2 Report Preparation for Legal Proceedings

  • Investigation reports intended for court must follow a structured format with clear separation of facts, observations, and professional opinions
  • All statements of fact must be supported by referenced evidence (photographs, documents, witness statements)
  • Reports must clearly identify the qualifications and experience of the investigator
  • Any limitations or uncertainties in the findings must be transparently disclosed
  • Reports must be reviewed by a senior investigator or QC reviewer before submission

7.3 Court Attendance

KEY Investigations provides investigators for court attendance, deposition, and cross-examination as required. Investigators are trained in court procedures, evidence presentation, and testimony best practices. Fees for court attendance are billed separately as agreed in the service terms.

8. Quality Control

All evidence undergoes quality control review before inclusion in investigation reports or submission to clients:

  • QC reviewers verify that evidence collection procedures were followed and documented
  • Photographic evidence is reviewed for clarity, completeness, metadata integrity, and relevance
  • Statements are reviewed for accuracy, completeness, and proper attestation
  • Chain of custody records are verified for completeness and consistency
  • Hash values are re-verified for all digital evidence before report finalisation
  • Any deficiencies identified during QC review are flagged via the Request for Information (RFI) process, and the investigating agent must address them before the report is released

9. Policy Review

This Evidence Handling Policy is reviewed annually and updated as necessary to reflect changes in applicable law, technology, industry standards, and operational experience. All personnel are notified of material changes and receive updated training as required.
